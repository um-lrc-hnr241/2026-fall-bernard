About Us

We are a student group exploring consciousness, freedom, and the ways people and objects shape one another. Our journals follow questions raised by our readings and lectures, including Descartes on the senses and self, Alfred Gell on art and agency, and Elaine Scarry on objects, bodies, and projection.

We come to these questions from different experiences and viewpoints. These reflections offer a snapshot of what each of us is thinking through.

Alejandro

Alejandro thinks of consciousness as an observer distinct from the physical self, and freedom as the ability to choose within limits. He connects discipline with self-understanding and the relationship between inner thought and the outside world. His reflections on Gell and Scarry consider how objects and decoration can carry agency and shape how we see reality.

“I believe that understanding is the key for conscious thought – that self-awareness is necessary.”

Leah

Leah began by understanding consciousness as thinking about one’s own thoughts and actions. Her experience of practicing tennis connects repetition with skill, confidence, and change. She is interested in how sensory experience and the agency people project onto art relate to consciousness, and how surroundings can influence who we become.

“Serving over and over helped build muscle memory, and improve my consistency and overall power.”

Robert

Robert describes consciousness as awareness of emotions, actions, and the body, while questioning how much decisions come from conscious choice or impulse. He sees personal identity as both continuous and changing. His reading of Gell led him to consider how a person’s mind and influence can extend through objects and relationships beyond their physical presence.

“When someone interacts with something you made, they are interacting with your mind, even if you are miles away or have been dead for centuries.”

Nidhi

Nidhi sees consciousness as connected to how we use knowledge and make decisions, and understands freedom as making choices without oppression. She is curious about what Gell means when objects related to a person have agency, and how that differs from an object simply being an heirloom. Her psychology studies also give her a scientific perspective on consciousness and the senses.

“What is the difference between an object having a bestowed agency and an object just being an heirloom?”

Our ideas are still developing. We use these questions to connect our readings with everyday experiences and to learn from one another’s perspectives.
